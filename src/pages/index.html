02 — How it detects

From file events to a decision you can read.

Detection is behavioural and deterministic. It does not look for known malware; it looks at what is happening to your files.

  1. OBSERVE

    Filesystem activity

    Creations, modifications, renames and deletions in your protected folders, from Windows file-change notifications.

    paths and timings, never contents

  2. EVALUATE

    Behavioural signals

    • Rapid modification +25
    • Mass rename +25
    • Suspicious extension +20
    • Mass creation +15
    • Event concentration +15

  3. SCORE

    Risk score

    The points of every signal over its threshold, summed. The weights add up to exactly 100.

    0–100 · per event, on arrival

  4. CLASSIFY

    Risk band

    The score maps to one of four bands.

    LOWMEDHIGHCRIT

    thresholds 30 · 60 · 80

  5. RESPOND

    Alert and incident

    HIGH raises an alert and opens an incident. CRITICAL also changes the security posture. You choose the response.

    recorded in the audit chain

03 — How it decides

One score. Four bands. A reason for every point.

Each signal contributes fixed points; the total is bounded at 100. With the default thresholds, no single signal can raise an alert on its own.

LOW1–29

Recorded with the event. Nothing is raised.

MEDIUM30–59

Events are marked for review on the Activity page.

HIGH60–79

An alert is raised; related alerts are grouped into one incident.

CRITICAL80–100

A critical alert, and the posture becomes CRITICAL THREAT while the incident is open.

What CRITICAL does — and does not do

CRITICAL is the most urgent state Sentinel reports: the dashboard's posture changes to CRITICAL THREAT and the incident is marked critical. It does not block programs, stop file changes or quarantine anything on its own. At every level, the response is yours.

Why it works this way

Ordinary bursts (saving many photos, extracting an archive, a sync client catching up) can look like an attack. A tool that acted automatically would act on those too. Sentinel shows its reasons instead, so you can tell the difference. What to do when an alert appears.

05 — Scope

Built for a specific job.

One layer of defence, not the whole of it. Use it alongside Microsoft Defender and tested backups.

It does

  • Behavioural filesystem monitoring of the folders you protect
  • Configurable protected folders, each reporting whether it is really watched
  • Risk scoring with the reason for every point
  • Incident recording in a tamper-evident local audit chain
  • Local operation: no account, no network connections

It does not claim

  • To detect every ransomware family. Slow or in-place encryption can stay below an alert.
  • To replace antivirus or EDR. Keep Microsoft Defender on.
  • Kernel-level protection. It runs in user mode, with no driver.
  • Cloud threat intelligence. It never connects to the internet.
  • To decrypt or recover files. Keep tested backups.
  • macOS or Linux support. The current release is for Windows.
